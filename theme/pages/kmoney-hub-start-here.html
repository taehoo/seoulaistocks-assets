Moving money, getting paid and paying bills in Korea follows a handful of rules that newcomers rarely find in one place. This desk collects them: the won exchange rate and what moves it, the Bank of Korea base rate that sets the tone for savings and loans, consumer prices, and the tax and health-insurance calendar that applies to foreign residents.

Every post is dated and links to the government page or data series it relies on. Rules change, so check the date at the top of a post before you act on it.

Posts in this series cover:

  • Banking and transfers — opening an account, identity checks, sending money abroad.
  • Tax and insurance — year-end settlement, the May filing month, national health insurance.
  • Prices and pay — inflation, the minimum wage and what they mean for a monthly budget.
