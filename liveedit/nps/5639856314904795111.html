Forty NPS Filings Landed on October 2, 2026

On October 2, 2026, the National Pension Service released 40 major shareholding reports detailing ownership shifts across multiple sectors of the Korean stock market[1]. The single-day batch showed selective accumulation alongside broader position trims. Equity increases totaled 14 filing entries while cuts accounted for 26 reports[2]. Every transaction was reported under the standard simple acquisition or disposal trade code.

The sheer density of public disclosures appearing on the same date highlights how the fund executes trades over time before bundling regulatory filings. Korean law grants a statutory window for filing after transactions settle, meaning these trades were completed across previous market sessions rather than executed on the day of publication. A heavy filing day reflects settlement timing rather than a sudden change in strategy.

Holding purposes recorded for these positions remain split between passive management and general investment. Kiwoom Securities, a financial services company, sits at 10.59% under a simple investment purpose[3]. Korea Aerospace Industries, an aerospace maker, stands at 7.08% under a general investment purpose recorded on August 27, 2025[4]. Hyundai Steel Company, a metals producer, holds a 9.84% stake registered under a general investment purpose since November 19, 2025[5].

Questions people ask

Why did the National Pension Service file 40 reports on October 2, 2026?

Korean regulation requires institutional investors to report major shareholding changes after trades settle. The pension fund submits aggregated disclosure filings periodically when positions cross reporting thresholds or change by 1 percentage point or more.

What Same-Day Disclosure Batches Hide From Investors

A single-day cluster of 40 filings published on October 2, 2026 reveals stake levels at specific reporting thresholds but cannot show intraday execution prices or counterparty identities[1]. Filings do not break down whether selling in companies like Paradise or Korea Circuit went to foreign institutional buyers or domestic retail accounts[2][18]. External exchange summary data shows that between September 3, 2026 and October 2, 2026, foreign investors recorded KRW -10 billion in net trading for Paradise while Korean institutions logged KRW 1 billion (≈$745,000)[41]. In Hanwha Machinery & Service Holdings, foreign net trading measured KRW -17 billion against KRW 3 billion (≈$2 million) from domestic institutions over the same period[41].

Disclosures also omit internal portfolio mandates and rebalancing triggers. A trimmed position in Cosmecca Korea or Hyundai Department Store reflects reported share counts on the filing date without indicating whether the fund was adjusting asset allocation weights or responding to price changes[19][21]. Furthermore, quarterly financial performance remains unconfirmed until earnings statements land. For instance, analyst estimates for Paradise anticipate Q3 2026 revenue of KRW 328 billion (≈$244 million) and operating profit of KRW 50 billion (≈$37 million), representing a projected 41% quarter-on-quarter operating profit rise from Q2 2026's actual KRW 36 billion (≈$27 million)[42]. Financial statements for that quarter have not yet been filed, leaving market watchers to verify actual operating metrics when official results publish.
