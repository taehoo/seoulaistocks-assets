This latest disclosure shows a minor increase of 0.08 percentage points from the previous filing, representing 2,008,118 shares held by the public pension scheme[1]. To put this position in perspective, we have to look back to the oldest record in our database from April 1, 2025, when the pension fund held a much larger 10.08% stake in the company. Over the course of eighteen months, the holding has been roughly halved, though the latest transaction represents a tiny step back above the threshold where public reporting becomes mandatory.

Under Korean market regulations, institutional investors are only required to make these disclosures when their ownership stake reaches 5% or more of a listed company's outstanding shares. When a holder dips below that line, they often disappear from the public disclosure stream entirely until their buying crosses the line again. That is exactly what happened here, making this latest filing a re-emergence of Hansae on the regulatory radar. As a private investor monitoring these filings from Seoul, I find these boundary-crossing moves particularly interesting because they force the fund to reveal transactions that would otherwise remain entirely invisible to foreign market observers.

Hansae Stake Rebounds Slightly to 5.02%

Hansae, an apparel maker, is now back in the five-percent disclosure club after the National Pension Service reported a 5.02% stake in its October 2, 2026 filing[1]. This is a notable shift from where the relationship began in our records, when the pension fund held 10.08% of the company on April 1, 2025. The gap between these two points represents a significant reduction in exposure, but the latest transaction shows the fund is not entirely finished with the stock. The minor addition of 0.08 percentage points was enough to trigger a fresh filing because it pushed the total ownership back over the statutory disclosure line[1].

My view is that this tiny transaction was likely a mechanical adjustment rather than a strategic repositioning. When a stake sits right on the edge of the 5% reporting threshold, even minor portfolio rebalancing or index tracking adjustments can trigger a brand new disclosure requirement. For foreign investors who cannot read the original Korean filings on the DART system, these frequent crossings can look like major decisions when they are actually just the byproduct of routine fund maintenance.

Chronology of the Pension Fund's Moves in Hansae

Hansae has seen its ownership structure revised by the pension fund through seven distinct filings over the past year and a half, showing a clear downward trend followed by a recent stabilization. The oldest filing in our database, dated April 1, 2025, showed the pension fund holding a 10.08% stake after trimming its position by 1.0 percentage point. This was followed by a more aggressive reduction on July 2, 2025, when the fund cut its stake by 2.08 percentage points to 8.0%. The selling continued into the autumn, with an October 1, 2025 filing showing a further drop of 2.93 percentage points, leaving the pension fund with a 5.07% stake.

April 1, 2025: 10.08%

The pattern reversed briefly at the start of the new year, when a January 2, 2026 filing showed the stake rising by 1.03 percentage points to 6.1%. However, this accumulation did not last, as the April 1, 2026 filing recorded a 0.3 percentage point reduction to 5.8%. By July 1, 2026, the fund had cut another 1.04 percentage points, bringing its reported stake down to 4.76%, which temporarily removed the company from the active disclosure list. The latest filing on October 2, 2026, marks the return to disclosure status with a 5.02% stake[1].

What the Purpose Record Shows

Hansae's latest filing does not show a holding purpose in our summary, though the DART report itself does state one. In the Korean regulatory framework, the holding purpose on record is a critical piece of information for assessing the fund's potential influence on corporate governance. Under the local rules, a 'simple investment' classification means the pension fund intends to remain a passive holder, whereas a 'general investment' classification indicates that the holder reserves the right to engage with management on governance issues. A switch between these categories is a signal of intent, not an action, but it serves as an important warning to the market about how active the pension fund might become.

Because the latest transaction was classified as a simple acquisition or disposal, we know the type of trade that occurred, but we must look directly at the full DART disclosures to track any formal changes in the fund's governance stance[1]. For a company like Hansae, which has seen its pension fund ownership cut in half over eighteen months, tracking whether the fund maintains a passive stance or reserves governance rights is essential for understanding the stock's institutional support.

Limits of What This Pattern Can Tell Us

This historical pattern of buying and selling cannot tell us whether the pension fund views Hansae as an attractive investment at its current valuation. It is a common mistake among foreign observers to assume that a pension fund's transactions reflect a direct valuation judgment or a directional bet on the company's business prospects. In reality, the fund manages massive portfolios that must constantly rebalance to match index weights, meet asset allocation targets, or fulfill specific external mandates. A series of sales, like the ones we saw throughout 2025, might simply be the result of the fund reducing its domestic equity exposure overall rather than a specific rejection of the apparel maker's fundamentals.

To illustrate how common these adjustments are across the market, the pension fund made several other disclosures on the exact same day. For example, on October 2, 2026, the fund reported increasing its stake in Foosung, a chemicals maker, to 6.22%, while simultaneously trimming its position in Hotel Shilla, a retailer, to 7.1%. These simultaneous moves across different sectors suggest a broad rebalancing process is constantly at play, making it impossible to isolate the fund's specific outlook on Hansae from these filings alone. The next concrete event to watch will be the release of the fund's next quarterly disclosure to see if the stake remains hovering around this critical five percent line.
